When part of a writing team, I adapt well to differing personalities. That does not mean I don't voice my concerns or issues, but I do know when to shut up and commit. I am often a leader in a group, but can easily step back when appropriate. I have no "sacred cows," as the saying goes.

When I think about some of my favorite people I've worked with, the qualities that they all held were empathy, curiosity, and holding themselves to a high bar.

As a solo writer, I've become accustomed to being my own project manager and to break down work into more achievable tasks. Now as a lead, I gauge team bandwidth with the breadth of work and divide as evenly as possible. As I learn a team's strengths and pace, I can adjust to ensure everyone is set up for success.

To me, success is defined in a few ways. There is completing tasks on time with high quality and maintaining that bar of quality. But I think the morale of the team is an important factor to success as well. For example, if we're burning out constantly to meet our goals, something is broken and needs to be addressed.

The following writing samples are from a section of documentation on a new SDK for game development. The content is written for not only engineers, but also product managers, producers, and other less technical cross-functional partners. This content was also going to be the model for our other SDK documentation.

Time was limited, subject matter experts didn't have a lot of time to collaborate, and the starting document was barebones and dependent on comments within the code. This was one of the first projects I wrote with an AI-assisted workflow I had been exploring.

I had written a set of skills to read through the code repo and explain how each piece of the SDK functions and works together. The output was bloated and had a few misinterpretations that I recognized and validated with the SMEs. After some heavy editing and rearranging content, I completed solid drafts of 7 documents in 5 days. These drafts included code samples, best practices for our users, and diagrams.

I used audit skills I defined to review the content for technical accuracy, readability for both human and AI, and style consistency. This greatly cut down on revisions with SMEs, with most edits being relatively minor.

I had some hiccups ensuring consistent results with the AI, but evolved my skills. With what I have learned since then, I would continue exploring how to augment my workflow. But the speed and depth I was able to learn about the more technical aspect of our product without taking up an SME's time was amazing. It resulted in a research skill I shared and used regularly to deep dive into the product as a solo writer and all-around-owner of the entire documentation experience.
